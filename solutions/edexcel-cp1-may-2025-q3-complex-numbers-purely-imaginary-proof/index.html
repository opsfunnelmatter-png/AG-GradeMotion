The Exam Archetype: The Purely Imaginary Conjugate Identity

Examiners do not invent new mathematics each year — they test consistent structural archetypes. In this paper, Q3 tests your ability to navigate the boundary between conceptual algebra and precise numerical computation.

Core Formulae Tested

$$w \in i\mathbb{R} \iff w + w^* = 0 \iff \text{Re}(w) = 0$$

$$z = x + iy \implies z z^* = |z|^2 = x^2 + y^2$$

Examiner Traps & Common Mark-Scheme Penalties

  • Assuming $z$ is real instead of expressing $z = x + iy$.
  • Sign errors during conjugate expansion $(a + ib)(a - ib) = a^2 + b^2$.

Algebraic Substitution and Conjugate Multiplication

If $|z| = 1$, the numerator becomes $2(1 - 1) = 0$, proving that $\text{Re}(w) = 0$ and therefore $w$ is purely imaginary.

$$w = \frac{z - 1}{z + 1} \implies w + w^* = \frac{z - 1}{z + 1} + \frac{z^* - 1}{z^* + 1} = \frac{2(|z|^2 - 1)}{|z + 1|^2}$$
